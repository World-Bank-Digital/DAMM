How to read this report

Evidence classes

M Measured — a number was recorded  D Documented — a citable artifact  J Judged — assessor statement without artifact  G Gap — searched for and not found. The class is derived from the recorded value, never chosen.

Levels & bands

Every indicator carries a level, L1–L5. A pillar band is the level the plain mean of its recorded levels rounds to: Nascent <1.5 • Emerging <2.5 • Established <3.5 • Advanced <4.5 • Transformative ≥4.5. Beside the band is a signed margin, the distance from the level the band is named for: +0.00 means the pillar sits squarely at that level, and a margin near ±0.5 means it is on the edge of the next one. Recorded gaps and levels withheld pending ratification are not averaged in, so where a pillar has any, the count of rated rows is printed beside the mean. A band in (parentheses) rests more on judgment, gaps and withheld levels than on levelled evidence.

Researched narrative · desk draft — cites scored rows; sets no level

Egypt's need profile inverts the usual reading: productivity is not the binding constraint. Cereal yields of 7,402 kg/ha (1.2) run at close to twice the world average, on land where 94.2% of the cultivated area is equipped for irrigation (proposed indicator, not scored), and agriculture value added per worker stands at $8,942 (1.1). The need concentrates instead in exposure and inclusion: cereal import dependency of 42.8% (proposed indicator, not scored) leaves the food system exposed to world markets, and the smallholder interface is thin — 6% contract-farming participation (1.6) and 2.3% rural formal borrowing (1.7), both below the lowest band the model defines.

What Egypt measures, it measures well: 20 indicators carry national statistics and 32 more rest on citable artifacts, with no row resting on unevidenced judgment. Five are recorded gaps — the gender balance of the digital-agriculture workforce (5.12), digital adoption among agribusiness SMEs (6.13), climate-advisory reach (8.17), a business-environment score for which Egypt's assessment cohort has not yet published (6.3), and rural mobile broadband coverage (2.1). That last one governs this report. No rural-specific coverage figure is published for Egypt by the regulator, the ministry or the ITU; the national figure is 99.8%, but a national figure does not evidence a rural prerequisite. Five indicators more carry evidence but no level, because what was found measures a different construct from what the indicator names — those are listed in the ledger and flagged for ratification.

Researched narrative · desk draft — cites scored rows; sets no level

Egypt's landscape is state-anchored: the Farmer Card is its spine — the mandatory channel for subsidized fertilizer, converted into a Meeza payment instrument — with FAO-linked digital extension (El-Mufeed), a state AI pest advisor (Hudhud) still at pilot reach, and a private cohort (Mozare3, FreshSource, Zr3i) that is real but thinly capitalized after the 2023 funding contraction.

The dominant structural overlap is in ADVISORY/EXTENSION CONTENT: at least four public or donor channels deliver farmer advisory in parallel — Hudhud (MALR/MCIT, AI), El-Mufeed (FAO-MALR), DAIRS (announced), GIZ AIP II online advisory — plus private channels (Zr3i, Yalla Mozare3), with no evidence of a shared content backbone even between the two government-linked apps that target the same extension service. Second overlap: FARMER REGISTRATION/ID — the Farmer Card registry, the fertilizer system's holding records, the MCIT holdings-automation project, and each donor project (SAIL) and startup (Mozare3) maintain separate farmer/beneficiary registries; the Farmer Card is the only asset behaving as shared infrastructure (registry + Meeza payments) that another system (fertilizer distribution) demonstrably reuses. Third overlap: SATELLITE CROP MONITORING is being built at least three times — the MCIT-MALR cropping-pattern program, FAO water-productivity/water-accounting work, and Zr3i — with no documented data-sharing between them.

Method — DAMM v1.7 in five lines

  1. Every indicator gets a value, a source, and a year — or a recorded gap. No level without a recorded value.
  2. The value sets the evidence class: number → Measured · citable artifact → Documented · neither → Judged · looked-for-and-not-found → Gap. Derived, never chosen.
  3. Pillars report a band and their evidence composition. No weights, no overall score, no stage.
  4. Prerequisites bind on presence only; a failed prerequisite blocks its use-case column, not a global stage.
  5. Source tiers (T1–T5) are reported, never weighted. Researched narrative cites scored rows; no narrative claim sets a level.

Automated quality control: 8/8 renderer checks passed (consistency, provenance, reconciliation and presentation — a failed check blocks the render). Human gates: G1, assessor confirmation of every machine-filled row — executed 22 August 2026, 18 derivations adjusted with recorded reasons; G2, independent peer review of all prerequisite and judged rows — executed 22 August 2026, all 12 prerequisite rows re-verified and confirmed; G3, task team leader sign-off against the four prohibitions — pending.

Prohibitions: no cross-country ranking · no band as a project development objective (PDO), disbursement-linked indicator (DLI), or disbursement condition · no automatic financing decisions · no public claim before human review.
