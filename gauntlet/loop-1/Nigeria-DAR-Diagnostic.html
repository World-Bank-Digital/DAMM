How to read this report

Evidence classes

M Measured — a number was recorded  D Documented — a citable artifact  J Judged — assessor statement without artifact  G Gap — searched for and not found. The class is derived from the recorded value, never chosen.

Levels & bands

Every indicator carries a level, L1–L5. A pillar band is the level the plain mean of its recorded levels rounds to: Nascent <1.5 • Emerging <2.5 • Established <3.5 • Advanced <4.5 • Transformative ≥4.5. Beside the band is a signed margin, the distance from the level the band is named for: +0.00 means the pillar sits squarely at that level, and a margin near ±0.5 means it is on the edge of the next one. Recorded gaps and levels withheld pending ratification are not averaged in, so where a pillar has any, the count of rated rows is printed beside the mean. A band in (parentheses) rests more on judgment, gaps and withheld levels than on levelled evidence.

Researched narrative · desk draft — cites scored rows; sets no level

Nigeria's need profile is a productivity story before it is a technology story: a third of the workforce (1.3) produces cereal yields of 1,558 kg/ha (1.2), under two-fifths of the world average, while one person in five is undernourished (8.1). Two proposed context indicators sharpen the picture in opposite directions. Cereal import dependency is 16.3% (proposed indicator, not scored) because the staple basket is domestic — the wheat exposure that dominates the headlines is real but commodity-specific. Irrigation, meanwhile, equips 0.3% of agricultural land, leaving those yields hostage to rainfall.

The evidence base is deeper than the country's reputation for data scarcity suggests: post-harvest losses carry APHLIS cereal estimates of 9.5–17.4% (1.5), women's land rights the SDG 5.a.1 statistic of 31.6% (8.5), mobile money the 2025 Findex wave at 32.8% (8.4), foundational ID coverage 79.1% of adults (4.7), and rural connectivity a genuine rural figure of 48% 4G coverage (2.1). What remains unmeasured is farmer-facing: the gender balance of the digital-agriculture workforce (5.12), language coverage of digital services (8.11) and climate-advisory reach (8.17) are recorded gaps, and seven further indicators carry evidence but no level because what was found measures a different construct from what the indicator names. Nigeria measures its systems better than it measures their users. The binding constraint, though, is not evidence but the last mile: rural electricity reaches 23.5% of rural residents (2.9), and that single universal prerequisite caps every use-case column at Partial, however strong the rails above it.

Researched narrative · desk draft — cites scored rows; sets no level

The landscape is crowded and discontinuous: the two largest farmer-facing programs (GES, ABP) are closed, NAGS-AP has taken over input support with the same registration-in-a-silo pattern, the national digital agriculture strategy has waited unadopted since 2020, and NIDAP — the vehicle meant to harmonize them — is early in rollout.

Farmer registration remains the register's structural overlap, and it has deepened since the starter pass. Closed programs enrolled millions in silos — GES 4.5M+ (program-reported), ABP ~4.67M (CBN-reported) — and the pattern is repeating: NAGS-AP has been re-registering farmers since 2023 (NIN-validated, ~650k reached per AfDB in Phase 1; 80k wheat farmers for 2025/26 alone), while private platforms claim millions more (ThriveAgric 1.2M+, Crop2Cash 500k+ — vendor-reported, and the Tingo fraud case shows such counts can be fabricated outright). Yet the national registry (indicator 3.3) records only a fraction of farming households. The government now concedes the fragmentation explicitly: the National Digital Farmers Registry (NDFR, Nov 2025) was launched precisely because — in the Minister's framing — duplicated, inconsistent databases have caused resource leakage. NDFR is the announced fix (NIN-anchored, NIMC-backed), but as of Aug 2026 it is a design-stage commitment with no published farmer count; NAGS-AP's NIN-validated rolls are the closest thing to a functioning registry and are program-owned, not national. Enrollment keeps happening; a registry still has not.

Method — DAMM v1.7 in five lines

  1. Every indicator gets a value, a source, and a year — or a recorded gap. No level without a recorded value.
  2. The value sets the evidence class: number → Measured · citable artifact → Documented · neither → Judged · looked-for-and-not-found → Gap. Derived, never chosen.
  3. Pillars report a band and their evidence composition. No weights, no overall score, no stage.
  4. Prerequisites bind on presence only; a failed prerequisite blocks its use-case column, not a global stage.
  5. Source tiers (T1–T5) are reported, never weighted. Researched narrative cites scored rows; no narrative claim sets a level.

Automated quality control: 8/8 renderer checks passed (consistency, provenance, reconciliation and presentation — a failed check blocks the render). Human gates: G1, assessor confirmation of every machine-filled row — executed 22 August 2026, 19 derivations adjusted and 1 class downgraded with recorded reasons; G2, independent peer review of all prerequisite and judged rows — executed 22 August 2026, all 12 prerequisite rows re-verified and confirmed; G3, task team leader sign-off against the four prohibitions — pending.

Prohibitions: no cross-country ranking · no band as a project development objective (PDO), disbursement-linked indicator (DLI), or disbursement condition · no automatic financing decisions · no public claim before human review. Rendered by the DAMM v1.7 engine and report pipeline from the scored indicator set and the initiative register for Nigeria; the same pipeline renders any country’s assessment. Source tiers are assigned by the DAMM Source-Tier Protocol lookup.
